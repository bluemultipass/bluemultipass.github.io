If you use the latest Anthropic models, you'll see output that cost nontrivial amounts of cognition to parse and understand. 



It frequently invents nouns and verbs requiring readers to unnecessarily make new associations. 



It attention baits with sentence structures like: "Not X, But Y", "N things, M of them matter" - which causes readers to automatically exert energy to identify implications and connect the dots. But rarely is there ever any to identify. There isn't a worthwhile ranking of issues. There isn't any contrast worth considering. 



It constantly writes long responses, filled with irrelevant information, forcing you to sift through garbage for what's useful.



If you modify your CLAUDE.md to constrain its behavior, it might follow it for a few rounds, but invariably, it will revert to its natural behavior in sessions longer than a few rounds. If you remind it, it just forgets again in a few rounds, maybe even the very next. In sessions using a 1M context window, I notice it exhibiting natural behavior as early as at 100k token usage. This makes the useful portion of the session very limited.



People have suggested asking it to essentially translate its response to be more readable. But when most of its responses need this treatment, it's like running with weights on your legs. Why am I subjecting myself to this when there are better alternatives?


If you use Claude all day as a software engineer, the exertion adds up and it becomes exhausting. It's gotten so bad that I'm now using Codex instead of Claude Code as my default.
